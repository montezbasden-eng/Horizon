## [How Complex Systems Fail: A 1998 Essay Still Resonates](https://how.complexsystems.fail/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The 1998 essay 'How Complex Systems Fail' by Richard I. Cook is being widely shared and discussed again, particularly on Hacker News, where practitioners highlight its enduring relevance to modern engineering practices such as chaos engineering. This essay challenges the conventional wisdom of root cause analysis in complex systems, arguing that failures are inevitable and often result from multiple interacting factors rather than a single cause. Its insights are crucial for engineers and organizations designing resilient systems in fields like software, healthcare, and transportation. The essay emphasizes that complex systems run in a degraded mode, with many flaws and redundancies, and that 'failure-free operations require experience with failure.' It also notes that post-accident reviews often reveal a history of 'proto-accidents' that nearly caused catastrophe.

**Background**: Complex systems, such as transportation, healthcare, and power generation, are inherently hazardous and subject to multiple failure modes spanning technical, human, and organizational dimensions. Resilience engineering, a related field, focuses on how systems cope with surprises and emphasizes maintaining redundancy and flexibility to survive upsets.

## [Fable and the End of the Free Lunch in AI](https://www.dbreunig.com/2026/08/23/fable-the-end-of-moore-s-law.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The article argues that the era of free performance gains in AI is ending, with diminishing returns and increasing costs, using the model 'Fable' as a case study. It highlights a shift where frontier models no longer offer the same cost-performance improvements as before. This matters because it signals a potential slowdown in AI's rapid advancement, affecting developers, businesses, and users who rely on ever-cheaper and more capable models. The debate over cost-performance trade-offs and safety limitations will shape future AI adoption and pricing strategies. The article references specific models like Fable, GPT-5.6, and Deepseek v4 flash, noting that cheaper models may offer sufficient performance for many tasks. Community comments highlight that safety limitations in models like Fable can make them less practical than slightly more expensive alternatives like GPT-5.6.
